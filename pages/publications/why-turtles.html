Why Turtles?

Popular Science Evolution
Date to be added

When we think of turtles, we simply imagine a sluggish, inferior creature that simply has made it through the eras due to the shell it cowardly decides to hide in when frightened. Except, while most animals are perpetually adapting to their environments, turtles are ingenious creatures who solved their problems 260 million years ago!

They started as a regular old reptile, when they interestingly decided to grow their bones thicker and form an outer skull. Imagine a tiny lizard slowly but steadily building itself a suit of armor, one bone at a time, without ever going to the blacksmith! Over millions of years, that armor kept creeping further around its body, first wrapping the belly like a shield strapped on underneath, then finally sealing up the back until the whole creature was zipped snugly inside its own personal chainmail.

Unfortunately, everyone assumed turtles were just weird, scaly cousins of snakes and lizards. Wrong! Turtles are secretly part of a much wilder archosaur lineage, the same one that gave us crocodiles, birds, and dinosaurs, with proof hiding right in their bones. Hidden deep in their skulls and ears are tiny clues, almost like fingerprints left behind by ancient relatives, proving turtles belong with the giants of the prehistoric world instead of the lowly reptiles everyone mistakes them for.

So, the turtle was never a coward hiding from danger, but an extremely intelligent creature optimizing for survival.
