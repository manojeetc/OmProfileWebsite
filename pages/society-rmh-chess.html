RMH — A Journey to Meet Yourself

Bringing weekly chess evenings to the largest Ronald McDonald House in the country, so families going through the toughest days of their lives could travel, for a while, into a world of puzzles and riddles.

OVERVIEW

How it started

On a chilly evening in November 2025, my dad drove me to the biggest Ronald McDonald House in the USA, in Columbus, Ohio, to pitch chess as a volunteering activity.

I was nervous. I already had a lot of coaching hours behind me from teaching kids in Tanzania and Uganda, but here I would be meeting people who were there for unfortunate reasons. I kept asking myself whether I was emotionally equipped to handle those encounters.

The team listened patiently while I explained why I loved chess and why I believed it could help residents take their minds off their current struggles. I could tell I had not fully convinced them — they liked the passion but were unsure the residents would reciprocate. Still, they gave me a chance: one weekly evening slot, starting January 2026.

STORY

The first game

On day one I walked in with my green tournament bag, and the staff showed us to the dining area. My parents set up a table with a satin tablecloth and posters explaining the event. I finished setting up the board, sat down, and waited.

Then came an announcement over the RMH speakers: a chess champion was in the dining area, and anyone could come to challenge or learn. That announcement made me feel responsible. I left my insecurities behind, because I was in a place where simply having a healthy life may be one of the biggest rewards there is.

  • After 15 minutes, a gentleman walked over and asked for a game — we played and discussed strategy for the rest of the evening.
  • He shared that one of his twins was in treatment at Nationwide while the other waited, and that seeing me reminded him of his own childhood.
  • Some weeks there were queues to play, and kids would wait an entire week just for the session.
  • Parents would come over to tell me how their kids loved chess or played on their school team.

Walking back to my dad's car that first night, I realized I had changed a little, and that my understanding of life had matured.

REFLECTION

What I learned

Showing up every week is hard alongside homework, olympiads, and tournaments. What keeps me going is being able to offer support in my own language — chess — to families in the middle of the hardest time of their lives.

After about four months, RMH told us they had received feedback that residents loved the activity. Going from a tryout to a well-formed program has been deeply satisfying, and I expect RMH will remain the most enriching experience of my chess journey.
